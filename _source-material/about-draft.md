## What I changed in paragraph three, and why

**The structure was the real problem.** Rev 2 read *"A lot of the work is brand and commercial…
The rest is narrative and documentary."* Two separate failures in one construction: "a lot of the
work is" is loose, and **"the rest is" grammatically demotes everything after it.** You noticed
the effect; the cause was the sentence shape.

Rev 3 uses **parallel construction** — *"The commercial side runs from… The narrative side runs
from…"* Same verb, same weight, neither subordinate. It reads as two halves of one career rather
than a specialty plus leftovers.

**Short-form labelling removed entirely.** Cut both *"Short-form, mostly"* and *"the kind of
thing that has to land in ninety seconds or not at all."* The second was the best-sounding line
in rev 2, and it was actively working against you — a producer weighing you for a series reads
that as *this person cuts commercials.* Sharp writing that argues the wrong case is worse than
plain writing that argues the right one.

**Long-form is now signalled by what's listed, not by claiming it.** The paragraph ends on
*"series and hour-long documentary"* — the longest formats on your resume, both true. That
positions you as someone who already works at length, which is far stronger than saying you'd
like to. Nothing in the copy caps your scope now.

**Tense smoothed in paragraph two.** Your *"After more than a decade of this, I've recently cut"*
mixed a long span with a recent event in one clause. Split into two sentences: the Oscar trailer
is recent, the sizzle count spans the decade. Both your facts, less friction.
